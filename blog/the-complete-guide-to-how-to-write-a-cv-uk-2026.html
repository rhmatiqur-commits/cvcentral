The Complete Guide to How To Write A CV UK (2026)

CV Central 05 August 2026 6 min read

What Is How To Write A CV UK?

Writing a CV in the UK is an essential skill for job seekers looking to make a great first impression on potential employers. A CV, or curriculum vitae, serves as a personal marketing tool, summarising your education, employment history, skills, and achievements. It helps prospective employers understand your qualifications and suitability for the role you are applying for. Understanding how to write a CV UK is crucial in today’s competitive job market, where a well-crafted CV can set you apart from other candidates.

The structure and content of a CV may vary depending on the industry, but the core purpose remains the same: to highlight your strengths and experiences in a clear and concise manner. In this comprehensive guide, we will delve into best practices, tips for beginners, and common pitfalls to avoid, ensuring you create a compelling CV that resonates with hiring managers in 2026.

Why How To Write A CV UK Matters

A well-constructed CV is vital for several reasons. First and foremost, it is often the first point of contact between you and a prospective employer, making it imperative to create a positive impression. In the UK, employers typically spend only a few seconds skimming through CVs, so having a clear, well-organised document is essential to capture their attention.

Secondly, the CV is tailored for the job you want, demonstrating your understanding of the role and how your experience aligns with the company’s needs. In crafting a CV that highlights key achievements and relevant skills, you effectively communicate your value proposition to employers.

Lastly, with the rise of digital applications, a CV reflects your ability to present information professionally and succinctly. In 2026, when applying online, being able to articulate your qualifications in a polished manner remains crucial, as automated systems often filter applications based on keywords and formatting.

How to Get Started with How To Write A CV UK

To begin your CV-writing journey, define your job target clearly. Identify roles that interest you and research the specific skills and experiences required. This step will guide the content and structure of your CV, ensuring you align your qualifications with the job description.

Before diving into writing, collect all the necessary information including:

  • Personal details (full name, contact information)
  • Professional summary or objective
  • Work experience (job titles, company names, dates of employment, key responsibilities, and achievements)
  • Education (schools attended, qualifications obtained, dates)
  • Skills (technical, soft, and language skills)
  • Additional sections (certifications, volunteer work, interests)
Once you've gathered your information, choose a CV format. The three most common formats to consider include:
  1. Chronological CV: Lists your work experience in reverse chronological order. This format is ideal if you have a solid work history in one field.
  1. Functional CV: Focuses on skills and experience rather than work history. This is beneficial for those with gaps in employment or changing careers.
  1. Combination CV: Merges both chronological and functional formats, showcasing skills while detailing work history. This is effective for showcasing both experience and transferable skills.
Crafting a CV can feel daunting, especially for beginners. But with a clear strategy and the right tools, creating a standout CV becomes achievable.

Best Practices for How To Write A CV UK

When it comes to writing a CV in the UK, adhering to best practices can dramatically enhance your chances of landing an interview. Here are some essential tips to follow:

  1. Tailor Your CV: Every job application should include a tailored CV. Adjust your CV to reflect keywords and skills mentioned in the job description. This tailoring shows that you genuinely understand the role and have the qualifications necessary.
  1. Use a Professional Format: Keep your CV neat and professional. Use standard fonts (like Arial or Calibri), an appropriate font size (10-12 points), and consistent headings. Leave white space to make it easier to read.
  1. Start with a Personal Profile: A compelling personal profile or statement at the beginning of your CV can provide a quick preview of your qualifications and career goals. It should be concise (2-4 sentences) and include essential attributes relevant to the job.
  1. Quantify Achievements: Where possible, use numbers to quantify your achievements. Instead of saying "responsible for sales," say "increased sales by 30% within six months." This concrete evidence of your contributions stands out to employers.
  1. Keep It Concise: Generally, a CV should not exceed two pages. Be succinct and only include information that is relevant to the position you are applying for.
  1. Proofread Carefully: Spelling and grammatical errors can be detrimental. Always proofread your CV multiple times, and consider asking a trusted friend or career advisor to review it for clarity and accuracy.
  1. Professional Online Presence: With the increasing importance of digital platforms, include links to your professional social media profiles (e.g., LinkedIn) if they bolster your application, but ensure they align with your CV's information.
By implementing these best practices, you are well on your way to crafting a commendable CV that stands out in the competitive UK job market.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Writing a CV can be fraught with pitfalls. Here are some common mistakes to avoid to ensure your CV remains professional and appealing to employers:

  1. Using an Unprofessional Email Address: Make sure your email address is straightforward and professional. Avoid using nicknames or unprofessional terms.
  1. Overloading with Information: Including too much detail can overwhelm employers. Stick to relevant experiences and avoid extraneous information.
  1. Neglecting to Update Your CV: Regularly update your CV to reflect your most recent experiences and skills. An outdated CV can misrepresent your current capabilities.
  1. Ignoring Formatting: A cluttered or poorly formatted CV can deter hiring managers. Ensure your CV is easy to navigate, with clear sections and headings.
  1. Being Generic: Avoid using vague language and generic descriptions. Tailor each CV to the specific job you are applying for, highlighting relevant skills and experiences.
  1. Making it Too Personal: While personal interests can add character, it’s vital to keep them professional and relevant to the job. Stick to interests that may demonstrate transferable skills.
By steering clear of these common mistakes, you can create a CV that showcases your strengths effectively and leaves a lasting impression.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How long should my CV be in the UK?

Typically, a CV should be no longer than two pages. Focus on relevant experiences and details to keep it concise.

2. Should I include my photo in my CV?

In the UK, it’s generally not customary to include a photo. Focus on showcasing your skills and experiences instead.

3. Is it necessary to include references on my CV?

It's not mandatory to include references directly on your CV. You can state that references are available upon request.

4. How frequently should I update my CV?

You should update your CV every time you gain new experiences, skills, or accomplishments, especially before applying for new positions.

5. What’s the difference between a CV and a resume?

In the UK, the terms are often used interchangeably, but generally, a CV is more comprehensive and can include additional details on qualifications and experience compared to a resume.

6. Can I use a template for my CV?

Yes, using a template can be a helpful starting point, but ensure you tailor it to your needs and personalize the information to reflect your experiences.
